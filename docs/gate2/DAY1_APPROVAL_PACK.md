**Scoring sheet (per participant, per cell):**
```
SP = (SP1+SP2)/2        TP = (TP1+TP2)/2
HL = (HL1+HL2)/2        EW = (EW1+EW2)/2
CORE HPS = (SP+TP+HL+EW)/4                    [1–7]
IB-raw = (IB1+IB2)/2                          [higher = worse; gates use IB-raw]
AC-raw = (AC1+AC2)/2  (desk: AC1 only)        [higher = worse]
TRUST = (TR1+TR2)/2
NOVELTY = NV1                                 [reported, never scored]
ΔHPS = CORE(test) − CORE(comparator), within-subject
VETO: no pass if AC-raw > 4.5 OR AC-raw rises ≥ +1.5 vs control (real-site cells)
```

## B. Recommended flagship vantage (ONE)

**The Campidoglio Forum Overlook** — the public terrace/balcony behind Palazzo Senatorio on the Capitoline Hill (Via del Campidoglio / Via di Monte Tarpeo railing), looking **southeast down the long axis of the Forum**: Arch of Septimius Severus and the Rostra in the near field, Temple of Saturn's eight columns at right, Basilica Julia's footprint, the Via Sacra receding past the Temple of Castor and Pollux toward the Arch of Titus, Palatine rising to the right.

Why it beats alternatives on every criterion:
- **Historical interpretability:** the single richest legible ensemble in Rome — political core (Rostra, Curia direction), religious (Saturn, Castor/Pollux), commercial/judicial (Basilica Julia), processional (Via Sacra) in one frame. Maximum HL-test material.
- **Visual transformation potential:** today it reads as a rubble field; the reconstruction delta (marble facades, roofs, gilded statues, crowded Via Sacra) is the most dramatic then/now contrast available anywhere — ideal for testing whether legibility creates presence.
- **Layered Then/Now suitability:** elevated ~15–20 m oblique angle gives natural depth layering (foreground arch → mid basilica → far Titus) — exactly what 2.5D parallax needs; minimal foreground pedestrian occlusion because tourists below are *inside* the scene, not blocking it.
- **Lantern suitability:** wide panorama with many discrete aimable targets (each monument a reveal), from a fixed standing position at a railing — the ideal beam-sweep geometry.
- **B2/VPS later:** stable monumental geometry with high visual-feature density; a fixed, repeatable standing position (railing) makes coarse alignment and later anchoring tests reproducible; the same vantage serves B1 → VPG → B2 → AB without re-authoring.
- **Access (PROVISIONAL — not yet field-verified):** believed to be free public street-level access outside the ticketed Capitoline Museums / Tabularium route, with no ticket required. **"Open 24/7" is NOT asserted as established experimental fact.** Field Access Verification (below) must confirm before any Rome testing.

**Exact outdoor public test position (definition, pending field verification):** the stone railing of the terrace where **Via del Campidoglio meets Via di Monte Tarpeo, at the southeast flank of Palazzo Senatorio** (public street level, reached on foot from Piazza del Campidoglio; NOT inside the museums or the Tabularium gallery, which is a separate ticketed indoor viewpoint that we are explicitly not choosing). Camera orientation ≈ southeast (approx. 120–135°), eye height ≈ 1.6 m at the railing, terrace ≈ 15–20 m above the Forum floor. Expected field of view ≈ 60–75° horizontal: Arch of Septimius Severus near-left with the Curia beyond, Rostra below-center, Temple of Saturn columns near-right, Temple of Vespasian foreground-right, Basilica Julia mid-field, Via Sacra receding along the axis past Castor & Pollux toward the Arch of Titus and the Basilica of Maxentius mass at far left-of-axis.

**FIELD ACCESS VERIFICATION (required before Rome testing — added to Rome trip gate):** confirm on the ground: (1) unrestricted public access at the exact railing position; (2) practical testing hours (early-morning emptiness assumed, not verified); (3) any security/police restrictions on lingering or repeated sessions; (4) tripod/equipment constraints at the railing; (5) crowd behavior and turnover at the position; (6) whether repeated participant testing (multiple sessions/day over several days) draws intervention. Until verified, all Rome session planning treats access parameters as assumptions.
- **Crowds / line-of-sight stability:** moderate crowds at the railing but self-clearing; the sightline itself can't be blocked (everything below eye level). Weather is the only variable.

Runner-up (recorded, not recommended): Via Sacra ground level at the Temple of Saturn — more visceral "inside the ruins" feel, but requires paid timed entry, heavy occlusion, unstable positions; better as a *second* stop for B2 Rome sessions than as the Gate 2 control site.

**Decision needed:** approve this vantage (all ART-0 layers, ledger scope, and protocols are authored against it).

## C. Exact baseline/control condition (REVISED per founder correction — control fairness)

> **Control "C": best current ChronoWalk-style static/binary Then/Now — built from the SAME ART-0 reconstruction.** The ART-0 depth layers are flattened into a single full-frame reconstruction image; the user toggles/cross-fades between the current-state photo and that flattened reconstruction (binary then/now, no parallax, no layering, no aiming). Same ~90-second narration audio as the test cells, captions identifying the five key monuments.

**Held constant across C / B1 / Lantern:** historical claims (one ledger) · reconstruction content (identical ART-0 layers, flattened for C) · viewpoint & framing · image resolution · reconstruction fidelity · exposure time (3 min) · narration content and voice. **The only manipulated variable is interaction/presentation:** C = static/binary toggle · B1 = layered cinematic Then/Now (parallax/scrub) · B5 = Lantern aimed discovery.

**Documented residual confound:** flattening removes depth-layer separation, so C is necessarily a 2D composite of the same art. This is inherent to the manipulation (layering *is* what B1 tests), not an asset-quality difference — content and fidelity are pixel-identical. Any compositing artifact introduced by flattening will be QA'd to invisibility before sessions; if any visual difference beyond layering survives QA, it is logged as a confound in the analysis section of the results. The earlier idea of using third-party polished artwork for C is **withdrawn** (it would have confounded interaction with asset quality).

## D. X-VPG protocol (LOCKED pending approval)

- **Cells:** C (baseline above) · V1 (B1 cinematic layered Then/Now, ART-0 assets) · V2 (Lantern aimed-reveal, same ART-0 assets, same vantage, same claims).
- **N:** minimum 6 (exploratory, E1); preferred 8–10 (E2). Within-subject, all participants experience all 3 cells.
- **Order:** Latin square, 3 sequences (C–V1–V2 / V1–V2–C / V2–C–V1), participants balanced across sequences; assignment by rotation at booking.
- **Exposure:** fixed 3 minutes per cell (timer, soft cutoff), ≥10-minute washout between cells (neutral conversation, no debrief).
- **HPS timing:** full questionnaire immediately after each cell, before any conversation; NV1 included every cell.
- **Forced choice (end of session, exact wording):** "If you could keep only ONE of these three on a real trip to Rome, which one? Why — one sentence." Plus full ranking 1–3.
- **Comprehension check (after all cells + ranking):** 5 questions on the vantage content — identify 3 monuments from the current-state photo; state the function of 2 of them. Scored /5; analyzed against which cell the participant saw first (order covariate).
- **Attention conflict:** AC1 per cell (desk form); IB1/IB2 per cell — IB is co-primary for V2 per matrix R1.3.
- **Decision rule (per R1.6):**
  - **GO (ART-1 justified):** either V-cell ≥ +0.7 Δ (core HPS or HL) vs C, with IB-raw ≤ 3.5 and no AC anomaly, at n ≥ 6 → evidence E1/E2.
  - **ITERATE:** Δ between +0.4 and +0.7, or IB 3.5–4.5 → one interaction-only iteration (no new art), re-test once.
  - **KILL (art spend stopped):** both V-cells < +0.4 Δ, or second ambiguous → no ART-1 commission; program pivots per matrix.
  - Too-noisy: any cell SD > 2.0 → E1 only, extend N before deciding.

## E. X-D0 protocol (LOCKED pending approval)

- **Eval set: 200 cases**, authored against the flagship-vantage claim ledger, structured:
  | Category | n | Purpose |
  |---|---|---|
  | Core factual (in-ledger) | 60 | correctness + citation of ledger claims |
  | Interpretive/contested (ledger marks scholarly debate) | 40 | must present as debate, not fact |
  | Out-of-ledger traps (real history, absent from ledger) | 40 | must refuse/defer, not improvise |
  | Adversarial (false premise, leading, invented "facts") | 30 | must correct or refuse the premise |
  | Scope/refusal-appropriate (off-topic, modern, opinion) | 30 | graceful boundary behavior |
- **Answer-correctness rubric (0–2):** 2 = correct and complete per ledger · 1 = correct but incomplete/hedged where ledger is definitive · 0 = wrong. "Useful answer" = score ≥ 1 or an appropriate refusal in trap/scope categories.
- **Unsupported-claim rubric (the kill metric):** an unsupported claim = any historical assertion not traceable to a ledger claim ID (paraphrase allowed, substance must map). Counted per answer, graded by AI grader with **human (founder) spot-check of 20% + 100% of flagged cases**. Hedged speculation presented as speculation still counts as unsupported if it introduces non-ledger content as historical description.
- **Trust-calibration rubric (0–2):** 2 = correctly labels fact vs reconstruction vs scholarly debate per ledger metadata · 1 = correct content, missing epistemic labeling · 0 = misrepresents certainty (states debate as fact or vice versa).
- **Comparison:** identical 200 cases through (a) constrained pipeline (retrieval over ledger + refusal policy) and (b) same model unconstrained. Report deltas on all three rubrics — quantifies what the ledger buys.
- **Pass:** 0 unsupported claims AND ≥ 80% useful AND mean trust-calibration ≥ 1.5 (constrained pipeline). **Kill:** > 2 unsupported after 2 pipeline iterations → runtime free-form Q&A cut from product scope (pre-authored Q&A only). **Ambiguous:** 1–2 unsupported → one more iteration + 50 fresh cases; recurrence = kill. Evidence class: E0 (technical proof).
