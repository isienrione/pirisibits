# HISTORICAL_PRESENCE_SCORE_V0.md
ChronoWalk 2.0 — Gate 1 · August 2026

A practical measurement instrument for "historical presence." Used identically across all tracks so experiences can be compared. **No single arbitrary threshold (e.g., 70%) is the sole decision criterion** — decisions read the whole instrument (see GATE1_DECISION_TREE.md).

## Design principles

1. **Comparative, not absolute.** Every test compares ≥2 variants (or variant vs. control) with randomized order; presence claims are only meaningful relative to an alternative.
2. **Delayed as well as immediate.** Presence that evaporates in an hour is spectacle; we measure again at +24h.
3. **Never lead the tester.** No variant is described as "immersive/AR/special" beforehand; the script introduces every variant with the same neutral sentence ("Here is a way to experience this place. Take as long as you like.").

## Quantitative block (7-point Likert, asked after EACH variant)

1. "The place came alive for me." *(core presence)*
2. "I could understand what used to be here." *(legibility — the Forum question)*
3. "I could imagine the people who occupied this place." *(human presence)*
4. "I noticed details I would otherwise have ignored." *(perception shift)*
5. "I see this place differently now." *(transformation)*
6. "This felt meaningfully different from an audio guide / a video / photos." *(category distance — anchor varies by variant)*
7. "I would pay for this experience in another city." *(economic intent)*
Plus one forced choice at session end: **"If you could keep only one of the versions you tried, which one?"** — the single most decision-relevant datum.

## Qualitative block (open, recorded, coded afterward)

- "Describe the moment that affected you most. Where were you standing? What happened?" *(unprompted presence narratives are the gold signal — we count spontaneous sensory language: "I saw", "I heard", "they were around me")*
- "Was there a moment that broke the experience? What exactly happened?"
- "Explain to a friend what this place used to be." *(comprehension test — scored for accuracy against the claim ledger)*
- At +24h (message follow-up): "What do you remember most?" and "Have you told anyone about it?"

## Behavioral signals (observed, not asked)

- Dwell time at the stop vs. baseline visitors.
- Body behavior: did they turn/look up/move to where the content pointed? (presence expresses physically)
- Interaction repetition: did they re-trigger the reveal/scene unprompted?
- Completion and voluntary continuation ("can we do the next one?").
- Spontaneous photography/sharing of the experience itself.
- Interruptions handled: did they resume or abandon after a real-world interruption?

## What NOT to measure (v0)

- NPS (too coarse at n<50, contaminated by politeness).
- Star ratings of "the app" (there is no app; testers rate experiences).
- Time-on-screen as a positive (screen time can be *anti*-presence; only dwell-at-place counts).
- Historical knowledge quizzes (comprehension is measured by explanation quality, not recall trivia).

## Anti-bias protocol

- Testers recruited as travelers/locals-hosting-visitors, not friends of the project; mix ages and languages; no tech-industry majority.
- Variant order randomized/counterbalanced; interviewer follows a written script; interviewer never built the variant they administer where possible.
- Small n honesty: n=10–15 per cell yields directional signal only — we report distributions and quotes, never percentages with false precision.

## Scoring & use

Each variant gets: median of Q1–Q7 · forced-choice share · coded qualitative themes · behavioral counts. A variant "wins" a comparison when it leads on forced choice AND core presence (Q1) AND produces unprompted presence narratives — a triangulated read, not a single number. Kill criteria in each experiment doc reference this instrument.
