# THRESHOLD_EVOLUTION.md
ChronoWalk 2.0 — Gate 0 · August 2026

Evolution of the Threshold concept (the crafted presence peak at each stop). The Pack defines tiers A/B/C with "Hold to restore Rome" as an *example, not sacred*. This document evolves the tier system for 2026 reality.

## Design principle

A Threshold is **one deliberately engineered peak of Historical Presence per stop** — the moment the Presence Gap closes. Its form must be chosen per site, per device capability, and per what prototyping proves actually produces the feeling. The tier system is a *capability ladder with graceful degradation*, not a feature checklist.

## Tier A — Narrated Presence (robust baseline)

*Every stop. Every device. Offline. Phone in pocket.*

- Scene-structured writing (setup → arrival → peak → meaning), directed voice performance, layered ambient sound design.
- **Evolution for 2.0:** head-tracked spatial audio where hardware allows (AirPods-class): sounds of the past positioned in real space around the standing traveler. Degrades to stereo binaural on any headphones, to plain narration on speaker.
- Micro-synchronization with place: narration references what the traveler is actually facing ("turn toward the columns on your right"), driven by heading + position.
- Label: `established`; spatial layer `device-dependent`.

**Why baseline matters:** if Tier A alone doesn't produce "the place came alive" for a majority of testers, no camera trick will save the product. Tier A is the first experiment (see EXPERIMENTS_BEFORE_ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Tier B — Anchored Reveal (practical frontier)

*Flagship stops only (2–4 per city). Requires VPS coverage or site scan + capable device + daylight conditions.*

- Traveler is invited (never required) to raise the camera at a precise vantage point. The view aligns via VPS-class anchoring; a historically reviewed visual layer resolves over the present — ruins re-complete, colors return, a moment replays.
- Interaction candidates to prototype (successor of "hold to restore"): slow-press restore · time-scrub dial · walk-through threshold line · dwell-triggered auto-reveal. Choose by testing, not taste.
- Honesty rule: reconstruction confidence is expressed in the visual language itself (certain elements solid, hypothesized elements sketched/ethereal) — turning I2 rigor into aesthetic signature.
- Failure design is first-class: anchoring can fail in crowds/glare; the reveal must degrade to a fixed-orientation composite, then to Tier A, without shame.
- Labels: anchoring `established`/`needs prototyping` per site; reveal content `expensive` per site.

## Tier C — Deep Reconstruction (flagship experimental)

*1–3 sites per city. The showcase.*

- A photoreal or near-photoreal reconstruction moment — Gaussian-splat-class rendering or hybrid pre-rendered volumetric scene — of something that no longer exists (Pantheon interior across centuries; the Forum alive).
- May be experienced in place (anchored) or as a held "window" without precise anchoring — prototyping decides whether anchoring materially adds presence versus cost.
- Labels: `needs prototyping` + `expensive`; per-site asset budget must be modeled before commitment (I8 frugality).

## Tier selection is editorial, not technical

The Foundry assigns each stop its Threshold form during production: some places are *audio places* (the Forum at dusk needs voices, not overlays), some are *reveal places* (a facade with a lost element), few are *reconstruction places*. The engine is city-agnostic (I7); the tier assignment and calibration are content.

## What Thresholds are never

- Never a gate: skipping a Threshold never blocks the walk (I3).
- Never dishonest: no invented certainty for spectacle (I2, I6).
- Never tech demos: a Threshold that doesn't move the Historical Presence Score gets cut regardless of how impressive it is (I5).
