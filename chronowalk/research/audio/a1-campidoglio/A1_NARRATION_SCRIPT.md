## Script

> Stand here a moment. Below you lies the Roman Forum as it stood in the early fourth century — the state you see reconstructed here reflects roughly the years 312 to 320.
>
> That triple arch on the left is the Arch of Septimius Severus, raised in 203 to celebrate his Parthian campaigns; its relief panels depict those wars. Look closely at the great inscription: one line was re-cut after 212, when Geta's name was erased from his brother's decree — damnatio memoriae, the official forgetting.
>
> The eight grey granite columns nearer to you belong to the Temple of Saturn, one of Rome's oldest sanctuaries — and beneath it, the aerarium, the state treasury of the Roman people.
>
> The long arcades across the square are the Basilica Julia, home of the civil courts. And on the far horizon rises something brand new in this era: the vast vaulted mass of the basilica begun by Maxentius — evidence suggests it was the largest roofed hall the Forum had ever seen.
>
> Scholars still debate many details of what you see — where reconstruction is uncertain, we have left it quiet. What is certain is this: for a thousand years, this small valley was the center of the world.
