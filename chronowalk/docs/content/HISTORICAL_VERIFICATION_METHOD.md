# Historical Verification Gate — Pass 1 (7 contested claims + Phocas spot + era check)

Method: web-verified against primary-evidence descriptions and scholarly reference works (sources §13 per item; bibliographic list at end). Evidence classes: **A** primary (remains/inscriptions/literary/coins/fragments) · **B** modern scholarly reconstruction · **C** speculative/illustrative. C is never treated as A/B. Disagreement is preserved, not resolved by fiat.

## Item 1 — Temple of Concord elevation/facade (CON-01)
2. **Question:** Can the Tiberian Temple of Concord's facade/elevation be depicted in detail for c. 315?
3. **Primary evidence (A):** platform/podium remains under the Tabularium slope; the celebrated Augustan-period cornice fragment with scrolled modillions (Tabularium/Capitoline collection); plan evidence (wide cella broader than deep) from remains; coin images of the Tiberian temple (hexastyle front shown on sestertii).
4. **Scholarly reconstruction (B):** LTUR I s.v. "Concordia, aedes" (plan, fig. 153); Gorski & Packer ch. on Concord (detailed conjectural elevation); Claridge pp. on Concord (cautious: plan secure, elevation reconstructed); UCLA RomeLab notes facade follows generic rules where evidence fails.
5. **Conflicts:** G&P offer a detailed order/facade; Claridge and LTUR treat elevation as substantially conjectural; coin images are schematic and disagree in detail with fragment-based reconstructions.
6. **Confidence:** plan/mass HIGH · facade detail LOW.
7. **Narration-safe:** yes for function/plan/wide-cella oddity; no for facade specifics.
8. **ART-0-safe:** massing only.
9. **May show:** correct footprint (wide cella, hexastyle porch mass), podium height class, roofline mass consistent with coins.
10. **Must NOT show as certain:** column order details, entablature/pediment sculpture, bay rhythm, the G&P facade as "the" facade.
11. **Treatment: simplified/mass-only.**
12. **Narration:** "Beside it stood the Temple of Concord — its footprint is certain, unusually wide; how its front looked in detail is scholarly reconstruction."
13. **Sources:** Claridge, *Rome: An Oxford Archaeological Guide* 2nd ed. (2010), Roman Forum §, Temple of Concord entry; Gorski & Packer, *The Roman Forum* (CUP 2015), Concord ch.; LTUR I (1993), "Concordia, aedes," fig. 153; penelope.uchicago.edu encyclopaedia_romana "Temple of Concord" + "Cornice from the Temple of Concord"; romelab.etc.ucla.edu "Aedes Concordiae."

## Item 2 — Basilica Julia roof form (BJ-02)
3. **A:** footprint, steps, pier stumps, flooring (in situ); no surviving superstructure above lower elements; literary notices of courts/rebuilds.
4. **B:** G&P ch. 14 (CUP) reconstruct a raised central nave with clerestory; digitales-forum-romanum offers variant massing; older reconstructions (Hülsen-era) differ in upper-storey articulation.
5. **Conflicts:** clerestory presence/height, roofing configuration, and second-storey treatment differ across B-sources; only the volumetric envelope (two-storey arcaded facade + roofed central hall) is common ground.
6. **Confidence:** envelope HIGH · roof/clerestory detail LOW.
7. **Narration-safe:** courts/facade yes; roof detail no.
8. **ART-0-safe:** massing only above the second storey.
9. **May show:** two-tier arcaded facade (fragment-supported), plain hipped/ridged roof mass without articulated clerestory detail, generic tile texture at distance.
10. **Must NOT:** specific clerestory windows, truss expression, decorated attic.
11. **Treatment: depict facade normally; roof simplified/mass-only.**
12. **Narration:** "The long arcades of the Basilica Julia are well attested; the roof above them is reconstruction."
13. **Sources:** Gorski & Packer (2015) ch. 14 "The Basilica Julia" (cambridge.org/core/books/roman-forum); Claridge (2010) Basilica Julia entry; digitales-forum-romanum.de "Basilica Iulia"; penelope.uchicago.edu "Columns of Diocletian" (post-283 rebuild context).

## Item 3 — Temple of Divus Julius facade (DIV-03 part B)
3. **A:** podium core + altar niche remains in situ; Vitruvius *De arch.* 3.3.2 cites the temple as *pycnostyle* (close-set columns); coin images (aureus/denarius types of Octavian, 36 BCE) showing the temple front.
4. **B:** LTUR II "Iulius, Divus, aedes"; G&P reconstruction (hexastyle pycnostyle); Claridge entry (cautious on elevation; podium and rostra-front secure).
5. **Conflicts:** coin schematics vs Vitruvian spacing vs fragment scarcity; the 315 state adds uncertainty (post-Republican restorations poorly documented).
6. **Confidence:** site/function/rostra-front HIGH · facade form MODERATE-LOW (order/column count reconstructed; pycnostyle attested but for the Augustan build).
7. **Narration-safe:** site, cremation association, rostra ad Divi Iuli — yes; facade specifics hedged only.
8. **ART-0-safe:** conservative massing with generic close-set hexastyle front, no decorative program.
9. **May show:** podium mass closing the vista, frontal platform (rostra), plain temple front with close-set columns.
10. **Must NOT:** pediment program, specific capital order as certain, any claim the depicted front is documented for 315.
11. **Treatment: visually hedged (constrained mass + generic front).**
13. **Sources:** Vitruvius, *De architectura* 3.3.2; Claridge (2010) Temple of Divus Iulius entry; LTUR III (1996) "Iulius, Divus, aedes"; G&P (2015) relevant ch.; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Caesar (survey of the reconstruction debate w/ refs).

## Item 4 — Sidus Iulium / star on the temple (DIV-03 part A vs B, separated per founder)
3. **A (for the star's association):** Caesar's Comet of 44 BCE in literary sources (Pliny *NH* 2.93–94 quoting Augustus; Suetonius *Div. Iul.* 88); coin types showing star/comet with DIVVS IVLIVS; the 36 BCE temple coin type shows a star in/above the pediment.
4. **B:** standard numismatic literature reads the coin star as the sidus Iulium on the temple front; placement (pediment center vs acroterion vs symbolic badge on the image, not the building) is debated.
5. **Conflicts:** whether the coin star records actual architectural decoration or numismatic symbolism; and whether any such feature survived to 315.
6. **Confidence:** A-claim (iconographic association) HIGH · B-claim (architectural placement/form at 315) LOW. **The founder's warning is confirmed: A must not inflate B.**
7. **Narration-safe:** yes as coin/comet story, explicitly hedged for the building.
8. **ART-0-safe:** omit the star from the depicted facade.
9. **May show:** nothing star-specific on the building.
10. **Must NOT:** a star rendered on the pediment as fact.
11. **Treatment: omit (visual); story lives in narration.**
12. **Narration:** "Coins show a star over this temple — the comet Romans read as Caesar's soul. Whether the building itself wore that star, and whether it still did in the fourth century, we can't say."
13. **Sources:** Pliny, *Naturalis Historia* 2.93–94; Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 88; RRC/RIC coin types (star + DIVVS IVLIVS; temple type of 36 BCE); Claridge (2010) entry; Wikipedia "Temple of Caesar"/"Caesar's Comet" surveys w/ refs.

## Item 5 — Arch of Septimius Severus summit quadriga (SEV-04)
3. **A:** the standing arch (no summit statuary survives); Severan coin types depicting the arch topped by a chariot group — read in standard accounts as a **six-horse** gilded-bronze chariot carrying Severus, flanked by his sons on horseback; attic corner sockets consistent with lost bronze groups.
4. **B:** Brilliant, *The Arch of Septimius Severus in the Roman Forum* (MAAR 29, 1967) — the standard monograph — discusses the summit composition from coins; general accounts (World History Encyclopedia; Claridge) repeat the six-horse (seiuges) + flanking equestrian sons reading.
5. **Conflicts:** coins are schematic; four- vs six-horse readings exist across types; figure count/arrangement varies by die. Exact composition cannot be fixed.
6. **Confidence:** "a large gilded-bronze chariot group with imperial figures stood on the attic" MODERATE-HIGH · exact composition LOW. **Founder caution confirmed: do not default to a conventional quadriga; six-horse readings are supported.**
7. **Narration-safe:** yes, hedged to coins.
8. **ART-0-safe:** silhouette/mass-only.
9. **May show:** an indistinct gilded chariot-group silhouette on the attic (multi-horse, no countable detail), flanking equestrian silhouettes optional.
10. **Must NOT:** countable horses, identifiable figures, any "documented" composition.
11. **Treatment: visually hedged (silhouette). If silhouette proves distracting in ART-0 QA, omit summit statuary entirely — both options founder-approved paths.**
12. **Narration:** "Coins show the arch crowned by a gilded chariot — some dies suggest six horses, with the emperor's sons riding alongside. The bronzes are long gone; the coins are all we have."
13. **Sources:** R. Brilliant, *The Arch of Septimius Severus in the Roman Forum*, Memoirs of the American Academy in Rome 29 (1967); Severan aurei/denarii arch types (RIC IV); Claridge (2010) arch entry; worldhistory.org/article/502 (survey w/ refs); ancientromelive.org "Arcus Septimii Severi."

## Item 6 — Polychromy of THESE monuments, c. 315 (GEN-04)
3. **A:** surviving materials themselves: grey/pink granite Saturn columns (in situ); colored-marble veneer traces and pavement marbles across Forum buildings; gilded-bronze statuary attested epigraphically/literarily (e.g., the Phocas-column inscription's "blinking from the splendor of gold" for a gilded statue — late but indicative of the tradition); brick-faced concrete cores where marble was the facing.
4. **B:** modern polychromy scholarship establishes color as material-inherent (marbles, granites, bronzes) for imperial monumental architecture; painted stucco is well evidenced for earlier/other contexts; specific painted schemes for these 4th-c. Forum facades are NOT documented.
5. **Conflicts:** degree of *painted* (as opposed to material) color on 4th-c. marble facades is genuinely open; popular "brightly painted Rome" images are class-C speculation for these buildings.
6. **Confidence:** material polychromy (colored marbles, granite, gilded bronze accents) HIGH · painted-surface schemes on these monuments LOW · saturated generic paint DO NOT DEPICT.
7/8. **Narration/ART-0:** yes, within the split below.
9. **May show:** true material colors — grey/pink granite columns (Saturn), white/veined marble, restrained colored-marble veneer fields, bronze/gilded roof and statue accents, terracotta roof tiles.
10. **Must NOT:** saturated painted facades, invented figural wall painting, uniform "painted temple" look.
11. **Treatment: depict normally BUT material-true only ("colored by material, not by paint").**
12. **Narration:** "Forget both postcards: not blinding white, not carnival-painted — the color came mostly from the stones themselves: grey granite, veined marbles, and fire-gilded bronze."
13. **Sources:** in-situ materials (Saturn granite shafts); Claridge (2010) materials notes per monument; polychromy overviews (Brinkmann et al. exhibition literature) — used only to justify restraint, not schemes; CIL inscription of the Column of Phocas (gilded statue).

## Item 7 — Basilica Aemilia portico naming (AEM-01)
3. **A:** remains of tabernae + two-storey portico strip fronting the basilica; Augustan-era sources naming a Porticus Gai et Luci (Suetonius/Dio references to honors for Gaius and Lucius).
4. **B:** Digital Augustan Rome: "commonly identified as the Porticus of Gaius and Lucius"; the identification debate goes back to Van Deman-era scholarship (AJA 17, 1913); Platner–Ashby s.v. Basilica Aemilia treats the identification as probable-not-certain; LTUR discusses competing placements.
5. **Conflicts:** whether the porticus named in sources IS this structure, or stood elsewhere (attribution, not existence, is contested).
6. **Confidence:** structure (shops + portico) HIGH · the NAME attribution MODERATE-LOW.
7. **Narration-safe:** structure yes; name only as "often identified as…" — or omit name entirely (recommended for VPG scripts).
8. **ART-0-safe:** yes (structure).
9. **May show:** two-storey portico + shop row massing/facade rhythm.
10. **Must NOT:** caption it "Porticus of Gaius and Lucius" as fact.
11. **Treatment: depict normally; naming suppressed in captions.**
13. **Sources:** Platner & Ashby, *A Topographical Dictionary of Ancient Rome* (1929) s.v. "Basilica Aemilia" (LacusCurtius); Digital Augustan Rome, "Porticus Gai et Luci"; AJA 17.1 (1913) "The Porticus of Gaius and Lucius"; LTUR I "Basilica Aemilia (Paulli)."

## Item 8 — The Phocas-column location in c. 315
3. **A (significant find, changes the ledger):** the column shaft is 2nd-century work, recycled; **the socle/base was recycled from a statue monument of DIOCLETIAN — its earlier inscription was chiselled away for the 608 text** (base uncovered 1813; CIL VI 1200). The 608 dedication (or re-dedication) to Phocas by Smaragdus is epigraphically certain.
4. **B:** Platner–Ashby "Columna Phocae"; Hülsen (1906) Forum monograph §14: discussion of whether the column monument as assembled predates 608 (only the *dedication* to Phocas is fixed at 608 — hence "dedicated or rededicated" in survey literature).
5. **Conflicts:** whether a Diocletianic/Tetrarchic statue monument stood **on this exact spot** in 315 (the socle was *recycled*, findspot ≠ certain original location), and when the present column was first erected there.
6. **Confidence:** column absent in 315 as the Phocas monument HIGH (dedication 608) · "a Diocletianic statue monument stood exactly here in 315" LOW (reused material proves existence of such a monument *somewhere*, not its location).
7. **Narration-safe:** yes — this is now a *better* story: the base under the Forum's last monument was carved for Diocletian and re-carved for Phocas.
8. **ART-0-safe:** the area stays **visually neutral**: open pavement, at most generic small statue bases consistent with GEN-03's honorific clutter — no specific monument invented.
9. **May show:** neutral paving/ordinary statue-base clutter indistinct at viewing distance.
10. **Must NOT:** the Phocas column, a specific "predecessor column," or a labeled Diocletian monument on that spot.
11. **Treatment: omit/neutral (founder's instruction confirmed as the right call — and the evidence adds a narration gem without any visual claim).**
12. **Narration:** "The lone column you see today was the Forum's very last monument — dedicated in 608 AD, on a base originally carved for Diocletian and chiselled over. In our reconstruction's year it isn't there yet."
13. **Sources:** CIL VI 1200 (base inscription, full text extant); Platner & Ashby (1929) s.v. "Columna Phocae"; C. Hülsen, *The Roman Forum* (1906) §"Column of Phocas" (LacusCurtius); en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column_of_Phocas (survey w/ refs, incl. Diocletian-socle recycling).

## Era check — is c. 315 CE right?
- **Why 315 beats 300/310:** the Basilica of Maxentius (begun 306, finished under Constantine after 312) is the skyline's biggest single mass; at 300–310 it is a construction site or absent. The Decennalia five-column monument (303) is present from 303 onward either way.
- **Why 315 beats 320+:** nothing visible materially improves after ~315, while later additions we do NOT want to adjudicate (e.g., the Equus Constantini, attested 334, placement debated) start crowding in; staying at ~315 avoids new contested objects.
- **Structures that depend on the choice:** Basilica of Maxentius (must be post-~312 to show complete); Decennalia columns (post-303); all post-283-fire rebuilds (Curia, Saturn, Basilica Julia) — satisfied by any date after ~305.
- **Anachronism scan at 315:** none found in the current ledger; MAX-01's "newly completed" phrasing is the only date-sensitive claim and is defensible (completion under Constantine after 312 is the standard account; exact year not certain).
- **False precision:** yes, "c. 315 CE" overstates. **Recommendation: relabel the target as "early 4th century CE (depicted state ≈ 312–320)"** — same image, honest label. All ledger "Era 315" fields become "≈312–320."
